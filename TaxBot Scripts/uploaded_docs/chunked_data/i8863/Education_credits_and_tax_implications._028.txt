Chunk Name: Education credits and tax implications.
Summary: You can potentially increase your education credit and reduce your tax liability if a student includes part of certain scholarships or fellowship grants in their income. These grants must qualify as tax-free under specific rules and can be used for non-qualified expenses like room and board. By applying part of the grant to these other expenses, the student will include that amount in their gross income and may need to file a tax return.

Chunk Content:
. You may be able to increase an education  credit and reduce your total tax or increase your tax refund if the  student (you, your spouse, or your dependent) chooses to  include all or part of certain scholarships or fellowship grants in  income. The scholarship or fellowship grant must be one that  may qualify as a taxfree scholarship under the rules discussed  in chapter 1 of Pub. 970. Also, the scholarship or fellowship grant  must be one that may (by its terms) be used for expenses other  than qualified education expenses (such as room and board). The fact that the educational institution applies the  scholarship or fellowship grant to qualified education expenses  (such as tuition and related fees) doesn't prevent the student  from choosing to apply certain scholarships or fellowship grants  to other expenses (such as room and board). By choosing to do  so, the student will include the part applied to other expenses  (such as room and board) in gross income and may be required  to file a tax return
