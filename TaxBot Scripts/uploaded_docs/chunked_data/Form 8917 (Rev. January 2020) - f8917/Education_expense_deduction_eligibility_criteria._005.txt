Chunk Name: Education expense deduction eligibility criteria.
Summary: The tuition and fees deduction is available for qualified education expenses paid for a dependent student in the current year or the first three months of the following year, provided the taxpayer claims the student as a dependent. This deduction cannot be claimed if the taxpayer's filing status is married filing separately, if another person can claim the taxpayer as a dependent, or if the taxpayer's modified adjusted gross income exceeds $80,000 ($160,000 for joint filers).

Chunk Content:
. The deduction  is based on the amount of qualified education expenses you  paid for the student in the current year for academic periods  beginning in the current year or beginning in the first 3 months  of the following year. Generally, in order to claim the deduction for education  expenses for a dependent, you must have paid the expenses in  the current year and must claim the student as a dependent on  your current year tax return (the “Dependents” line of Form 1040  or 1040SR). For additional information, see Pub. 970. You can’t claim the tuition and fees deduction if any of the  following apply.  Your filing status is married filing separately.  Another person can claim an exemption for you as a  dependent on his or her tax return. You can’t take the deduction  even if the other person doesn’t actually claim that exemption.  Your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI), as figured on  line 5, is more than $80,000 ($160,000 if filing a joint return)
