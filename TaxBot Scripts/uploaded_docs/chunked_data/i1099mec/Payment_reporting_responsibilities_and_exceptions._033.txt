Chunk Name: Payment reporting responsibilities and exceptions.
Summary: Payments made on behalf of another person may require filing Form 1099NEC if the payor has management oversight or a significant economic interest in the payment. For example, a bank financing a construction project is responsible for reporting payments over $600 to contractors. However, certain payments, such as those made to corporations, do not require a Form 1099NEC, even if they are taxable to the recipient.

Chunk Content:
. 281. Payments made on behalf of another person. For payments reportable under section 6041, if you make a payment on behalf of another person who is the source of the funds, you may be responsible for filing Form 1099NEC. You are the payor for information reporting purposes if you perform management or oversight functions in connection with the payment, or have a significant economic interest in the payment (such as a lien). For example, a bank that provides financing to a real estate developer for a construction project maintains an account from which it makes payments for services in connection with the project. The bank performs management and oversight functions over the payments and is responsible for filing information returns for payments of $600 or more paid to contractors. For more information, see Regulations section 1.60411(e). Exceptions Some payments do not have to be reported on Form 1099NEC, although they may be taxable to the recipient. Payments for which a Form 1099NEC is not required include all of the following.  Generally, payments to a corporation (including a limited liability company (LLC) that is treated as a C or S corporation)
