Chunk Name: Reporting taxable income adjustments accurately.
Summary: When amending your return with Form 1040X, if your original taxable income was reported as $0 but you had a loss, enter the actual taxable income in column A, enclosing negative amounts in parentheses. If the result in column C is zero or less, you must enter 0 in that column. Properly reporting losses and additional income is crucial to avoid overstating your tax liability.

Chunk Content:
. Also see the instructions for Forms 8995 and 8995A for more information for figuring and reporting your qualified business income deduction. Line 5—Taxable Income If the taxable income on the return you are amending is $0 and you have made changes on Form 1040X, line 1, 2, or 4b, enter on line 5, column A, the actual taxable income instead of $0. Enclose a negative amount in parentheses. The amounts in columns A and B can be less than zero. However, if the result for column C is zero or less, you must enter 0 in column C. Example. You showed $0 taxable income on your original return, even though you actually had a loss of $1,000. You later discovered you had additional income of $2,000. Your Form 1040X, line 5, would show ($1,000) in column A, $2,000 in column B, and $1,000 in column C. If you failed to take into account the loss you actually had on your original return, you would report $2,000 in column C and possibly overstate your tax liability
